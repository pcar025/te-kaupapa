Pou 1: Whakapapa & Identity Safety

Focus:
Identity, whakapapa, whānau voice, cultural identity, intergenerational context, strengths, and cultural protective factors.

Use placeholder review language such as:
- Whakapapa information documented appropriately
- Whānau voice captured in notes
- Cultural identity discussed
- Strengths and intergenerational factors identified
- Protective cultural factors identified
- Practitioner demonstrated understanding of whānau context

Example reflective prompts:
- How did I identify and uphold the identity of this whānau?
- Did I reduce the person to symptoms or hold their wider story?
- What assumptions did I make about this whānau?
- Did I recognise strengths alongside distress?
- How did whakapapa influence my understanding of risk?
- Did I create enough space for whānau voice?
- What cultural disconnections may be impacting wellbeing?

Example safety flags:
- Notes focused only on deficits or problems
- Minimal cultural information recorded
- Clinical language dominates documentation
- Whānau strengths absent
- Identity or cultural distress not explored

Pou 2: Manaakitanga & Duty of Care

Focus:
Respectful communication, feeling heard and safe, appropriate follow-up, boundaries, responsiveness to distress, and escalation of safety concerns.

Use placeholder review language such as:
- Respectful communication evident
- Whānau feedback on feeling heard or safe
- Follow-up completed appropriately
- Boundaries maintained
- Responsiveness to distress documented
- Safety concerns escalated appropriately

Example reflective prompts:
- How did my presence impact this person?
- Did whānau leave feeling more diminished or more upheld?
- Did I rush, rescue, avoid, or over-control?
- Was my communication mana enhancing?
- Did I notice emotional shifts in the room?
- How did I balance aroha with professional responsibility?
- Did I respond to distress or avoid it?

Example safety flags:
- Missed follow-ups
- Delayed responses to risk
- Abrupt or clinical-only language
- Repeated boundary crossings
- Whānau disengagement after sessions
- Practitioner documenting frustration toward whānau

Pou 3: Tikanga & Ethical Practice

Focus:
Consent, confidentiality, ethical decision-making, tikanga considerations, consultation, and clear rationale for difficult decisions.

Use placeholder review language such as:
- Consent completed properly
- Confidentiality explained and upheld
- Ethical decision-making documented
- Tikanga considerations included in planning
- Appropriate consultation sought where needed
- Clear rationale documented for difficult decisions

Example reflective prompts:
- Did I uphold tikanga in this engagement?
- What ethical tensions existed?
- Did I avoid difficult conversations because of discomfort?
- Was informed consent genuinely understood?
- Did I honour both professional ethics and tikanga?
- Was power shared appropriately?
- Did I consult when I needed guidance?

Example safety flags:
- Missing consent documentation
- Ethical decisions poorly justified
- Practitioner acting outside scope
- Lack of consultation during risk situations
- Repeated complaints or confusion from whānau

Pou 4: Kaitiakitanga & Risk Management

Focus:
Risk assessment, safety planning, escalations, referrals, environmental risks, cultural safety interventions, crisis response, and visible/invisible risk.

Use placeholder review language such as:
- Risk assessment completed
- Safety plan documented or updated
- Escalations and referrals recorded
- Environmental risks identified
- Cultural safety interventions included
- Crisis response documented appropriately

Example reflective prompts:
- What risks were visible and invisible?
- Did I minimise or overstate risk?
- Did I avoid escalation because it felt uncomfortable?
- How did wairua distress show itself?
- Were safety plans realistic and relational?
- Did I include whānau and cultural supports in planning?
- What risks exist for the practitioner or team?

Example safety flags:
- Repeated incomplete risk assessments
- High distress with low intervention response
- No evidence of safety planning
- Multiple crisis events without reassessment
- Practitioner avoiding escalation processes

Pou 5: Puukenga & Practitioner Capability

Focus:
Practitioner capability, training, supervision, reflective notes, intervention capability, scope of practice, ongoing learning, uncertainty, and support needs.

Use placeholder review language such as:
- Training completed
- Supervision attended
- Reflective notes completed
- Intervention capability demonstrated
- Scope of practice adhered to
- Evidence of ongoing learning

Example reflective prompts:
- What knowledge gaps showed up for me today?
- Did I work beyond my capability?
- Where did I feel uncertain?
- What support or supervision do I need?
- How am I maintaining cultural integrity in practice?
- Am I becoming reactive, burnt out, or disconnected?
- What strengths did I bring into this session?

Example safety flags:
- Working outside scope repeatedly
- Avoidance of supervision
- Increased reactive language in notes
- Repeated medium to high risk cases without consultation
- Signs of compassion fatigue or burnout

Pou 6: Haepapa & Accountability

Focus:
Timely notes, reviews, reassessments, reporting obligations, supervision or hui attendance, follow-through on actions, whānau feedback, and transparency.

Use placeholder review language such as:
- Notes completed on time
- Reviews and reassessments completed
- Reporting obligations met
- Supervision or hui attended
- Follow-through on actions evidenced
- Whānau feedback included in reviews

Example reflective prompts:
- Who am I accountable to in this situation?
- Did I follow through on commitments?
- What impact did my actions have on trust?
- Have I been transparent in my documentation?
- Did I avoid responsibility anywhere?
- What conversations need to happen that I may be avoiding?

Example safety flags:
- Late or missing documentation
- Repeated incomplete tasks
- Lack of review updates
- Avoidance patterns in accountability
- Discrepancies between notes and actions

Pou 7: Oranga & Protective Factors

Focus:
Protective factors, whānau strengths, connection to supports, cultural engagement, wellbeing improvements, goal progression, mana restoration, and progress monitoring.

Use placeholder review language such as:
- Protective factors identified and updated
- Whānau strengths documented
- Connection to supports increased
- Cultural engagement evidenced
- Improvements in wellbeing tracked
- Goal progression documented

Example reflective prompts:
- What sources of strength are emerging for this whānau?
- Did I focus too heavily on problems?
- How is mana being restored?
- What indicators of oranga are visible?
- What has shifted emotionally, relationally, or spiritually?
- What protective factors still need strengthening?

Example safety flags:
- No strengths documented
- Intervention focused solely on crisis
- Whānau becoming increasingly isolated
- Goals not reviewed or updated
- Lack of progress monitoring
